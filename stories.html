GRADUATE JOURNEYS • VERIFIED OUTCOMES

They didn't just sit in class.
They built a future.

Every story below represents a real participant aged 13–20 who entered with curiosity and graduated with a verifiable trade credential, paid commercial clients, or an independent business.

Alumni: Fall 2025 Cohort
🔧 TRADES & ELECTRICAL APPRENTICE

Marcus Jackson, 17

"I came in just wanting something real to do after school. I left knowing how to wire an entire circuit breaker panel and inspect home wiring."

During his 6-week cohort, Marcus inspected electrical outlets for 12 elderly residents in city apartments under Master Electrician Dan Vance. Three weeks after graduation, he was signed to a paid union apprenticeship starting at $22.50/hr.

Alumni: Summer 2025 Cohort
👟 CREATIVE ENTREPRENEUR

Talia Rodriguez, 16

"I went from ruining sneakers in my bedroom to selling custom Nike Dunks for $180 a pair to real clients online."

In the Sneaker Lab, Talia learned proper acetone leather de-glazing, airbrush masking, and protective finishes. She launched her Instagram brand 'TaliaKicks' during Week 4 and booked 22 client commissions before the semester finished.

Now: Founder, TaliaKicks Custom Studio
Alumni: Spring 2026 Cohort
💻 DIGITAL MEDIA DIRECTOR

Maya Santos, 18

"Nobody would hire an 18-year-old video editor without a portfolio. Generation News gave me camera rigs and real commercial clients."

Maya shot three commercial spots for neighborhood bakeries and barbershops during her 4-week cohort. She now freelances full-time, charging $600 per video, while taking evening digital cinema classes.

Alumni: Fall 2025 Cohort
🍳 CULINARY STARTER

Devon Miller, 15

"Cooking at home is one thing. Prepping 120 taco plates under a 3-hour timer for paying neighbors taught me real commercial pressure."

Devon formulated his own habanero pepper hot sauce line during Week 3 of Culinary Basics. His sauce was bottled in our commercial kitchen, tested for shelf life, and sold out 80 bottles in 2 hours at the winter community market.
